# Grant Types & Funder Archetypes

When to use: Identify funder type before any other step — it determines register, evidence standard, and budget expectations.

---

## Government (Federal & Provincial)
- Formal, policy-aligned language; mirror RFP terminology back into the application
- Scored rubrics with explicit weights; hard eligibility requirements disqualify automatically
- Overhead typically capped 15–20%; detailed line items required; matching funds common
- Mistake: emotional/mission-driven language in a policy-driven application

## Community & Private Foundations
- Mission-driven, relational tone; program officers read holistically, not just by rubric
- Funding pillars matter more than policy alignment; prior relationships carry weight
- Simpler budgets; indirect cost rates often uncapped if justified
- Mistake: bureaucratic language; failing to connect to their specific pillars

## Corporate Giving Programs
- Business-friendly, outcome-focused; acknowledge co-branding and volunteer opportunities
- Brand alignment, geographic proximity, employee engagement are implicit criteria
- Smaller awards ($5K–$50K); recognition line items expected
- Mistake: ignoring geographic/sector fit; no mention of recognition

## Cross-Cutting Rules
- Always use the funder's exact vocabulary — never substitute synonyms
- Never apply government formality to a foundation application or vice versa
- If funder type is ambiguous, default to formal and flag for human review
