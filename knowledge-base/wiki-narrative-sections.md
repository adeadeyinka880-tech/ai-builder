# Narrative Section Frameworks

When to use: Structure and write each required narrative section. Each section has a specific job — not just content to fill.

---

## Problem / Need Statement
**Job:** Convince the funder the problem is real, significant, and the org is positioned to address it.
- Must include: who is affected and how many (quantified), root causes, geographic specificity, evidence, connection to funder priorities
- Failure: describing the solution here; using national stats without localizing; vague unquantified claims

## Project Approach
**Job:** Describe exactly what will be done, by whom, when, and why this approach works.
- Must include: specific activities and deliverables, timeline by phase, staff/partner roles, theory of change, evidence base for the approach, how it avoids duplication
- Failure: activities without rationale; vague timelines; claiming evidence-based without citing evidence

## Outcomes & Evaluation Plan
**Job:** Commit to measurable results and explain how they'll be tracked.
- Must include: outputs (produced), outcomes (changes), indicators, data collection methods, reporting plan
- Failure: confusing outputs with outcomes; unmeasurable outcomes ("feel empowered"); no data collection plan
- Flag: any outcome target → `[HUMAN: verify this target is achievable based on past performance]`

## Organizational Qualifications
**Job:** Prove the org can deliver what it promised.
- Must include: relevant past projects, key staff credentials, confirmed partnerships, infrastructure, financial management capacity
- Failure: irrelevant credentials; unconfirmed partnerships; generic capacity claims not tied to this project

## Budget Narrative
- Every line item justified in plain language — *why* it's needed, not just what it is
- Dollar figures must exactly match the budget spreadsheet — see [[wiki-budget-conventions]]

## Section Sequencing
Always assemble in RFP-specified order, not drafting order. Funder's sequence takes priority.
