# Budget Conventions

When to use: Build and justify the budget. Reviewers use the budget to assess financial management capacity. A budget that doesn't add up is a red flag.

---

## Standard Line Item Structure
- **Personnel:** Title, % FTE, annual salary, calculated cost. Format: `Program Coordinator — 0.5 FTE @ $55,000/yr = $27,500`
- **Benefits:** Separate line, typically 15–25% of salary. Never omit.
- **Consultants:** Role, rate (hourly/daily), estimated hours, total. Justify why contractor vs. staff.
- **Program/Direct Costs:** Itemize anything over $500; group smaller items by category
- **Equipment:** Items over ~$1,000–$5,000 may be capital — check RFP for eligibility rules
- **Travel:** Per-km at government rates, airfare estimates, hotel per diems. Justify necessity.
- **Overhead/Indirect:** Government grants often cap at 15–20%. Check RFP cap before calculating.

## Budget Narrative Rules
- Every line item gets one-to-two sentences explaining *why* it's needed (not just what it is)
- Dollar figures in narrative must **exactly match** the spreadsheet — no rounding
- After generating narrative, produce a **reconciliation table**: every dollar figure in narrative alongside its source line item
- Contradiction between org profile and budget → flag before proceeding

## Matching Funds & In-Kind
- In-kind: value at fair market rates (volunteer time → check Imagine Canada/Independent Sector rates)
- Confirm whether in-kind counts toward the match requirement — not all funders accept it

## Common Errors
| Error | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Narrative says $45K, budget shows $47.5K | Reviewer flags discrepancy — signals poor attention |
| Overhead exceeds funder cap | May disqualify or require budget revision |
| No benefits line | Unrealistic — reviewer assumes org doesn't know true costs |
| Capital items not flagged | May be ineligible |
| Revenue ≠ Expenses | Automatic red flag |
